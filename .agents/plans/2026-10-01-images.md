# Images

Status: **plan, 2026-10-01**, done. The spec is
`.agents/specs/images.md` (normative); this plan orders the work onto
the code as it stands at `b7e3dd9`.

## Scope

In: `<img>` and `<picture>` drawn on the grid — sizing as replaced
boxes, the picture sampled at one column by two rows of pixels per
cell, `object-fit` and `object-position` with the rounding rule, the
themes' palettes, posterizing and dithering, and animated images.
Out: `background-image: url()`, `<svg>`, `<video>`
and `<canvas>` (the spec's "Later").

## What it builds on

- **A layer without cells of its own**, its box over the grid's
  (`plain-text.ts` `openLayer`): later ink covers its cells (the
  layer's `holes`), an ancestor's clip and an enclosing layer apply
  (`paint.ts` `paintLayers`, `placeLayer`), and the pointer passes a
  covered cell (`paint.ts`'s hit walk skips holes) — the image's own
  fill and border staying the grid's ("Found on the way").
- **The picture is a `<canvas>` in the layer's box**, over the content
  box, its backing store one pixel per column and two per row, scaled
  up unsmoothed (`image-rendering: pixelated`). A covered cell is a
  1×2-pixel `clearRect`, the holes already in the canvas's own pixel
  grid.
- **Atomic inline boxes** (`tree.ts` `pushBox`, `isAtomicInline`) carry
  an inline image on its line; **aspect ratio** (`cell-model.md`
  "Aspect ratio") derives the other size; the **renderer leaf's**
  replaced sizing (`buildRendererLeaf`, `width ??= max-content`) is the
  precedent for intrinsic size.
- **The themes' palette generator**
  (`packages/themes/scripts/generate-palettes.mjs`) holds each theme's
  system palette and maps monochrome ones by lightness.
- **Tailwind's preflight** makes images `display: block`,
  `max-width: 100%` and `height: auto`, and the `width`/`height`
  attributes compute an `aspect-ratio: auto w / h` the engine already
  reads: an image with its attributes has its ratio before it loads.

## A correction to the spec

**Monochrome themes map an image by lightness**, as their tokens do
(the generator's `mono` flag): green phosphor, amber and teletype
match each pixel's OKLab lightness alone, not its color, or a red
apple on a green screen would pick a green by chroma. The generator
emits `--mw-image-match: lightness` beside `--mw-image-palette` for
those themes, and the spec says so. The palette is a property of its
own, registered `<color>+ | none`, so the browser computes each color
whatever syntax the theme or author wrote (the engine's color reader
takes computed forms alone); a keyword can't share its syntax, hence
the second property.

## Milestones

Each step's tests are seen failing first.

### 0. Probes — done 2026-10-01

In Chromium, Firefox and WebKit (the pinned Playwright builds), in
throwaway pages:

1. **Hiding the native pixels**: `opacity: 0`, a `content` of a 1×1
   transparent GIF, and `object-position: -99999px` each hide them
   and keep the hit target, a link around the image (its click), and
   the `alt` in the accessibility tree, in every engine; `content`
   and `object-position` leave the computed opacity the author's, and
   `drawImage` still draws the source under either, `naturalWidth`
   too. **`content` is the lock**: it says what it means — no native
   picture — whatever the author's `object-fit` and `object-position`,
   and `placeLayer`'s live opacity read stays right.
2. **Sampling** a 4000×3000 picture to 80×80 pixels: the exact box
   filter over the full bitmap takes 90–165 ms; the browser's own
   downscale is fast but rough (Firefox up to 122 levels off, its
   two-step 52). **Halving in the browser** while the picture is at
   least four times the target (each halving at exactly half size, an
   exact 2×2 average in every engine), then the exact box filter in
   JS: 4–15 ms, within 5 levels of the exact average everywhere. No
   worker.
3. **`ImageDecoder`** is in all three, `isTypeSupported` true for GIF,
   PNG, WebP, AVIF and JPEG; a two-frame GIF's frames and durations
   (100 and 200 ms) decode same-origin and cross-origin with CORS.
4. **The light element's box**: the locked image never shows (probe 1,
   at the box's centre, after load).

### 1. Reading and sizing (`tree.ts`, `style.ts`, `observed.ts`) — done

- An `<img>` builds a leaf with an `image` record on its node: the
  element, its natural size, `object-fit`, `object-position`, and the color
  settings (`--mw-image-palette`, `-match`, `-levels`, `-dither`,
  inherited).
- Its intrinsic width is the natural width on the spacing scale
  (`pxToCells`), its height derived from its ratio — the natural one
  where `aspect-ratio` is `auto` (CSS's) — as `w-[Npx] aspect-[…]`
  sizes a box; its width `max-content` where unset, as a replaced
  box's. An `<img>` whose
  `display` is `inline` is an atomic inline box (`childRole`,
  `pushBox`).
- Loading: sized by its attributes or CSS, the box at once; unsized,
  no box; failed, its `alt` as inline text.
- A capturing `load` and `error` listener on the host lays it out
  again; `srcset` and `sizes` join `observed.ts`.
- Tests (`test/images.test.ts`, happy-dom with `naturalWidth` stubbed):
  each sizing case of the spec's; inline, block, flex item; the
  rounding flag; loading, loaded, broken.

### 2. The picture (`image.ts`, pure) — done

Functions over pixel arrays, so Node tests them exactly:

- the placement: `object-fit` and `object-position` over the pixel
  grid, `fill` drawn as `cover` where rounding sized the box;
- the box filter from a source bitmap to the pixel grid, over the
  browser's halvings (probe 2);
- posterizing to n levels; the nearest palette color in OKLab, or by
  lightness; the 4×4 Bayer dither, anchored to the picture's own pixel
  grid (stable when it moves); Floyd–Steinberg.
- Tests (`test/image.test.ts`): each on known bitmaps, against
  hand-computed pixels.

### 3. Paint (`plain-text.ts`, `paint.ts`, `styles.css`) — done

- An image opens a layer of no cells at its content's place in the
  paint walk ("Found on the way"); its layer's box takes a `<canvas>`
  over the content box. The canvas is drawn from a cache keyed by the
  picture's inputs (the spec's "Repaint"), so a layout that changes
  none of them draws nothing.
- A readable image is sampled through `image.ts`; a tainted one is
  drawn by `drawImage` straight to the pixel grid, unreduced.
- Covered cells cleared; the native pixels hidden by the `content`
  lock (probe 1); the layer's opacity the author's.
- Stories (`stories/images.stories.ts`, `Features / Images`): the
  spec's list — inline in a sentence, the same size with its `width`
  and `height` attributes, `object-cover` and `object-contain`, a
  caption over an image, an image in a scroller and in a rotated
  layer, a cross-origin image, a broken one's `alt`. The pictures are drawn by
  `stories/assets/generate.mjs` and imported; the tainted one is the
  same file from the server's other name (`localhost` and `127.0.0.1`).

### 4. Color: themes and utilities — done

- `utilities.css`: `image-palette-none`, `image-posterize-{2..8}` and
  `image-posterize-[n]`, `image-dither-{ordered,diffusion,none}`, and
  the four inherited properties registered.
- The generator emits `--mw-image-palette` in each theme's palette
  file, and `--mw-image-match: lightness` for the monochrome ones.
- Stories: one image under every theme, posterized, and each dither.

### 5. Animation — done

- Where probe 3 allows: the image's bytes decoded by `ImageDecoder`,
  each frame reduced and drawn on its canvas at its own time, a timer
  per animated image stopped when its node leaves the layout or the
  host disconnects; no layout per frame. First frame alone otherwise
  and under `prefers-reduced-motion` (its change observed).
- A story-only test: the frames advancing into a second pass, and
  stopping when the image is removed or the host disconnects (and
  running again once it is back). Reduced motion is a twin with
  `matchMedia` stood in for, as no engine sets the preference from a
  page; happy-dom has neither `ImageDecoder` nor a 2D context, so the
  spec's Node tests of the frames are the stories'.

### 6. Docs and verification — done

- The spec's status, the correction above, touch points in the
  present tense; `cell-model.md` and `generated-content.md` where they
  say an image draws natively or not at all; `gradients.md` deviation 3
  pointing to the spec's "Later"; core's and the themes' READMEs.
- Unit tests per package; the image stories in three engines, then on
  Linux; goldens scoped to `features-images--`, then the whole visual
  run; `pnpm check` (its log read) and `pnpm test`.
- **Benches**: the change touches reads and paint. A page with no image
  must not move (the image path is one tag test per element); a new
  bench shape with a dozen images, timed against itself as the
  baseline for later work.

## Found on the way

- **A layer root is a stacking context**, which paints in the positioned
  step, after the in-flow content: an image made one would have
  painted over a positioned caption earlier in the document. The
  picture is instead a step of the paint walk of its own
  (`stacking.ts` `picture`), at a replaced element's content's place
  (CSS 2.1 Appendix E), which opens a layer of no cells there; the
  image's fill and border stay the main grid's.
- **An image's opacity is its group's** when the picture opens inside
  it, so `placeLayer` multiplies the live element opacity in for a
  layer root alone.
- **Part-covered pixels** read as grey bands along a contained
  picture's edge: the place snaps to whole pixels (`pictureGrid`).
- **An inline image's baseline is its bottom**, a replaced box's; the
  engine reads an image's `vertical-align` whatever its display.
- **The palette dither's spread** at 255/∛n checkered every flat area
  of a VGA or C64 picture; half of it keeps the period look and the
  flat areas flat.
- **A shrink-to-fit host undersized `w-*` content** (not the images'
  own): it measured its light DOM natively, where `w-24` is 96 px, and
  laid it out at 24 columns, so a flex row's host was the width of its
  text and its `w-24` box overflowed it — or an image, with Tailwind's
  `max-w-full`, narrowed to it. Fixed on its own: the host's intrinsic
  widths are now the engine's (cell-model.md "Host sizing").
- **A CORS-shared image loaded without `crossorigin`** (picsum.photos,
  as tried in the playground) is tainted, so its theme colors and
  posterizing were left out. The engine reads its bytes itself over
  CORS, as for an animated image's frames, and draws its still from
  them; the canvas the browser's fallback drew on, unreadable to the
  page from then on, is replaced by a fresh one. The stories' servers
  share a file asked for `?shared` and nothing else (Vite's CORS off),
  so a shared and an unshared cross-origin image test the same
  everywhere.
- **A selection didn't show over an image** (seen in the playground):
  the grid's highlight lay under the picture. A selected image is
  reverse video like selected text — its cells inverted by drawing
  alone (white's difference, the alpha restored from a copy; WebKit
  ignores a canvas `filter`), so a picture the page may not read
  inverts too: whole for a light-DOM selection, the spanned cells for a
  grid one, whose `selectionchange` redraws the pictures alone. A real
  drag over a picture still showed nothing: the browser extends a drag
  into the `<pre>` under the pointer, the picture layer's grid of
  blanks, so the selection spanned two grids and its end missed the
  picture's cells; that grid is left empty, the drag landing in the
  grid's text beneath.
- **Clicks and copy, as the user chose** (2026-10-02): a triple-click
  selects an image, its whole a leaf's as `<mono-ascii>`'s
  (`#leafContents`); a grid-mode double-click its row of cells, a
  grid unit of the same gesture (`#unitUnder`, `pictureRow`), so the
  engine owns its release as for any gesture — a row the shortcut set
  apart was collapsed by the browser's mouse-up where the press landed
  in a selection. A grid-mode copy reads its cells with its alt
  written in them (`altLines`), from a render of its own: the grid
  draws none — the first try held it there as hidden text, which a
  selection showed in an `object-contain` picture's bare bands — so the
  engine copies a grid selection over a picture, and an element copy
  reads the image's box, its border's glyphs too, from that render. A
  text-mode copy took the same cells at first; the user set it right:
  text mode copies the alt alone, in the flow. A range around an
  inline image, its parent's points, copied nothing: a point just past
  an atomic box mapped to the text before it, no run standing for the
  box, and now maps past its marker. A select-all copied the
  browser's text, no alt in it, in either mode: the user chose the
  host's part as the engine writes it, the page's text around it. The
  listener moved to the document (a select-all's copy targets the
  body), the range is read in its composed points (its own ends inside
  the host), and in grid mode Chromium's select-all from inside the
  grid holds the shadow viewport whole, which counts as the host.
- **A select-all inverted no picture** (the user's, in the
  playground): in grid mode its range runs across the viewport's
  grids, and the picture read only a span with both ends in one grid;
  in text mode, Chromium's and Firefox's range holds the document,
  ends outside the host, and the engine painted only a range with both
  ends inside, text included, though wide-characters.md says a
  select-all reaching in is painted. A picture reads the selection's
  part in its grid, and the paint and the copy the part inside the
  host (`hostPart`).
- **A triple-click lit the rows past an image first in its host**
  (the user's, in Chrome): the range on the host itself, Chromium
  paints through the flat tree, the grid's own highlight with it, seen
  where the picture leaves the grid bare — the next paragraph's row, a
  border. The grid is unselectable under a semantic selection.
- **A selected image's copy took its border, its highlight not** (the
  user's): the copy reads its content box, as the highlight does,
  reversing the earlier copy of its border's glyphs.
- **From the second review**: an image that is itself a layer root
  (`grayscale`, `rotate-3`) threw at paint, its surface keyed as its
  own layer, now marked a picture and keyed apart; a hidden image drew
  and copied its alt; a copy read past its clip into the next box's
  cells, and a layer-root image's alt from the wrong grid; a
  percentage-sized image kept its natural min-content, which held a
  shrink-to-fit host wide, where CSS gives none; `auto <ratio>` kept
  the attributes' ratio past the natural one; a still read from its
  bytes stayed coarse as its box grew; a new source blanked the
  picture until it loaded; a palette's ordered dither offset by a
  quarter of a guessed step, dithering midtones alone, now by its
  colors' average step to their nearest (VGA's 85, black and white's
  255); a broken image's
  multi-click selected nothing; the grid copy kept an alt's raw white
  space; a select-all's page text took hidden text — another host's
  metrics probe — and no `<br>`, walking the page twice. The sampling
  and the reduction allocate nothing per pixel now.
- **From the third review**: the surface of an image that is itself a
  layer root took its effects again inside its own layer's, now none;
  a percentage-sized image gave way at min-content only once loaded,
  past its min width; a broken image drew its alt's raw white space;
  a select-all copied another host's part as raw page text, now as
  that host copies it, and flattened preformatted page text and kept
  unselectable text; Shift on a picture's row threw across the trees;
  a range ending past a link holding an image dropped its alt; a paint
  rebuilding the rows a grid selection ends in read the selection from
  before it, now as it stands, its span once a grid; a row of a
  clipped image ran past its clip. The images-in-layers story fits its
  host now: its row overflowed by two columns, so its scroller's width
  was the flex shrink's and the golden moved with it.
- **Who shaped a box, for `fill`** (the user's question): a flag set
  where the author gave both sizes or a ratio missed every other shape
  of theirs — a `max-h-*` clamp drew `fill` as `cover`. The rule reads
  the box's geometry now: within half a cell of the image's ratio is
  the rounding's shape (`pictureFit`).
- **A text-mode drag copied nothing in the playground** (the user's):
  the engine cancels the press to drive the drag, which also cancels
  the focus move, so the preview's frame never took focus and Cmd+C
  went to the editor's. The engine blurred only a control inside the
  host; it now blurs whatever held focus and focuses its own frame.
- **`object-fill` with an `aspect-*` covered** (seen in the
  playground): the rounding rule took the box for the engine's unless
  both sizes were set; an `aspect-ratio` of the author's own (not
  `auto`'s, the attributes') shapes it too, and `fill` stretches.
- **A painted selection could outlive its removal** (one Firefox run in
  four of the image's text-selection story): Firefox fires one
  `selectionchange` for a selection and its removal together, and a
  paint between them (a layout's) drew the selection, which the lone
  event, finding none, never repainted away — selected text as much as
  an image. The engine remembers whether its last paint drew a
  selection, not whether the last event found one: none in sixteen
  runs since, and a story swallowing the first event, as Firefox's
  coalescing does, fails without the fix every time.
- **From the review**: an animated image off screen or under a hidden
  host drew every frame, so a frame waits for its picture to show (an
  `IntersectionObserver` per animated image); a still image's whole
  file was read and copied into a decoder to learn it doesn't animate,
  so the decoder is fed the response's stream, its header enough, and
  the bytes are read only where it animates; Firefox reports a closed
  decoder's rejected `completed` as unhandled (seen with a plain
  `fetch` of a large PNG, ten times in ten), so each decoder's is
  handled; the picture's canvas is `aria-hidden`, as the grid is; and
  the reduction skips a transparent pixel, whose black error diffusion
  carried into its opaque neighbours.
- **Engines' `ImageDecoder`s differ**: WebKit counts an endless loop
  as `-1` (the spec's `Infinity`) and throws closing a closed decoder;
  Firefox shares a frame's image between decodes of its index, so a
  frame decoded again after one of its index closed is broken. The
  loop decodes each frame once a decoder, in order, a fresh decoder on
  each pass, and keeps the frame showing at its picture's scale to draw
  again on a repaint, each decoded frame closed once read (one at its
  natural size may weigh tens of megabytes).

## Risks

- **Sampling a large image on the main thread.** One sample per
  picture change, cached, 4–15 ms for 12 megapixels (probe 2).
- **Every image a layer.** A page of a hundred thumbnails is a hundred
  layer boxes and canvases. Cheap to place; the bench's image shape
  measures it.
- **An SVG `<img>` with no intrinsic size** (probed): every engine
  reports 300×150 px for one with no `viewBox` either, as CSS's default
  object size; for one with a `viewBox` alone, its ratio at 150 px
  wide (Chromium, WebKit) or 300 (Firefox), where CSS fills the line —
  the spec's deviation 3, as no read tells the two apart.
- **The canvas in goldens**: Chromium in the pinned Linux image draws
  it deterministically; the other engines' runs stay goldenless, as
  every non-Chromium visual test is.
